Can you actually afford this EMI?

Lenders use a simple rule: all your EMIs together should stay under about 40–50% of your in-hand income. This gauge applies it to your current loan.

Tight — doable, but watch it

Your EMI of ₹19,410 is 32% of a ₹60,000 income. That leaves ₹40,590 a month for everything else, and about ₹4,590 of headroom before you hit the 40% line.

Max EMI at 40%: ₹24,000 Headroom: ₹4,590 / month

This is a rule of thumb, not a sanction. Your lender will also look at your credit score, existing EMIs, job stability and the loan's purpose. Never borrow to the maximum the bank offers — borrow to the maximum your life can carry.

The moratorium — read this twice

During your course (plus 6–12 months after), you pay no EMI. But interest keeps adding up on the full amount. On a ₹15 lakh loan at 9.5%, two years of moratorium adds roughly ₹3 lakh to what you owe before the first EMI. If you can, pay just the interest during the course — it keeps the loan from ballooning.

Good news: families with annual income under ₹4.5 lakh can claim the interest subsidy (CSIS) for the moratorium period on education loans for technical and professional courses in India. Ask your bank about it before you sign.
